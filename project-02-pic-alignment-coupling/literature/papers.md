# Literature Basis — Alignment-Tolerant Fiber-to-PIC Coupling

Stage 0 deliverable. Screening of candidate papers, selection of the primary
reference, and the reasoning behind the choice.

Screening date: 2026-10-03

---

## 1. Screening criteria

A paper was only considered *reproducible* for a Zemax system-level model if it
reports enough of the following that the optical model can be built **without
inventing geometry**:

| # | Criterion | Why it matters for a Zemax POP model |
|---|---|---|
| C1 | Wavelength | Sets the diffraction scale; POP is monochromatic per run |
| C2 | Source mode size (MFD or waist) | Defines the POP Beam Definition |
| C3 | Receiving mode size | Defines the POP Fiber Data (receiver) mode |
| C4 | Lens geometry (ROC, material, thickness) | Defines the Lens Data Editor rows |
| C5 | Propagation distances / working distance | Defines surface thicknesses |
| C6 | Nominal coupling loss | Baseline for Stage 4 validation |
| C7 | Measured 1-dB tolerance (lateral / axial / angular) | Target for Stage 5 validation |
| C8 | Simulation method disclosed | Tells us what we are reproducing |

A paper that is topically relevant but fails C2, C4 or C7 cannot be used as the
primary reference, however well cited it is.

## 2. Candidates screened

### P1 — Mangal, Snyder, Van Campenhout, Van Steenberge, Missinne (2021) **[PRIMARY]**

> "Monolithic integration of microlenses on the backside of a silicon photonics
> chip for expanded beam coupling," *Optics Express* **29**(5), 7601–7615 (2021).
> DOI: 10.1364/OE.412353

| C1 | C2 | C3 | C4 | C5 | C6 | C7 | C8 |
|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|----|
| ✅ 1310 nm | ✅ 4.6 µm waist radius | ✅ TEC + SMF | ✅ ROC table vs substrate thickness, Si | ✅ substrate thickness is the distance | ✅ | ✅ lateral + angular + longitudinal | ✅ OpticStudio Gaussian propagation |

The only candidate that scores on **all eight** criteria. Decisive points:

- It states the grating-coupler output as a **Gaussian beam with a 4.6 µm waist
  radius (9.2 µm diameter) at 1310 nm** — which is literally a POP Beam
  Definition entry, not something we have to assume.
- Its design table (expanded beam diameter and required microlens ROC vs silicon
  substrate thickness) was itself **generated by Gaussian beam propagation in a
  model built in OpticStudio**. We are therefore reproducing a published
  OpticStudio result with an independent OpticStudio model — the cleanest
  possible validation target for this project.
- Both architectures are characterised: beam without the lens (diverging in the
  substrate) and beam with the lens (collimated, 32 µm diameter).
- The lens material is **silicon**, which is in the Zemax material catalog. No
  assumed index.
- Tolerances are reported on **three axes**, including angular — which is the
  axis where the micro-lens is expected to get *worse*, and therefore the axis
  that makes this a real research question rather than a foregone conclusion.

### P2 — Mangal et al. (2019/2020) **[PRIMARY COMPANION]**

> "Expanded-Beam Backside Coupling Interface for Alignment-Tolerant Packaging of
> Silicon Photonics," *IEEE JSTQE* **26**(2), 1–7.

Same group and architecture, earlier/parallel reporting. Supplies numbers P1
abbreviates: Si microlens ROC of **480 µm** for collimation, a measured **700 µm
1-dB longitudinal tolerance** into expanded-core fiber, **±2.5 µm** 1-dB
microlens-to-grating placement accuracy, and **34 µm** beam diameter over a
630 µm combined substrate thickness. Used as a cross-check on P1, not as a
separate model.

### P3 — Gradkowski & O'Brien (2024) **[ANALYTICAL VALIDATION]**

> "Tolerances of the alignment of fibers to photonic integrated circuits,"
> *Applied Optics* **63**(32), 8407–8412 (2024). DOI: 10.1364/AO.540682

Not a device paper — a derivation. It solves the alignment of two Gaussian beams
in closed form and gives single-axis tolerance equations, confirming the
normalised result that the −1 dB lateral offset is ≈0.48× the beam radius.

This is the project's **third validation leg**. Most student projects validate
simulation against one experiment. We will validate against (a) a published
experiment and (b) closed-form Gaussian overlap theory, independently. If Zemax,
theory and experiment agree, the model is trustworthy; if Zemax agrees with
theory but not experiment, we have located the physics the model omits.

### P4 — Gradkowski & O'Brien (2025) **[DESIGN EQUATIONS]**

> "Chip-agnostic optical packaging of photonic integrated circuits using
> refractive micro-optics," *Applied Optics* **64**(14), 4014–4022 (2025).
> DOI: 10.1364/AO.557834

Governing equations for designing the expand-and-collimate micro-optic (ROC and
thickness from a target expanded MFD). Gives us a *physically motivated* starting
point for Stage 9's parameter study, rather than sweeping blindly.

### P5 — Scarcella, Gradkowski, Carroll, Lee, Duperron, Fowler, O'Brien (2017) **[BENCHMARK]**

> "Pluggable Single-Mode Fiber-Array-to-PIC Coupling Using Micro-Lenses,"
> *IEEE Photonics Technology Letters* **29**(22), 1943–1946 (2017).
> DOI: 10.1109/LPT.2017.2757082

Dual micro-lens array, 1300 nm, **1.7 dB insertion loss (68% efficiency)**,
**±30 µm 1-dB tolerance** — the "order of magnitude relaxation" headline result,
Zemax-simulated. **Rejected as primary**: it is a four-page letter and does not
publish lens prescriptions (ROC, material, thickness). We would have to invent
the lens, which §3 of the brief forbids. Retained as the upper benchmark the
discussion must address: a ±30 µm tolerance implies a collimated beam of
≈125 µm MFD, and our model should predict that from first principles.

### P6 — Rejected candidates (logged for completeness)

| Paper | Why rejected |
|---|---|
| Free-form 3D-printed couplers (arXiv:2312.13329) | Free-form surface prescriptions not published; cannot be built in a Lens Data Editor |
| GRIN couplers (IOP *J. Phys. Photonics*) | Graded-index profile, not a refractive surface; different modelling class |
| Lensed-fiber / SSC edge couplers | Tolerance is set by nanoscale taper physics — outside the Zemax boundary set in §5 of the brief |
| Ball-lens backside interfaces | Useful context, but beam is not well described by a single Gaussian after a thick ball lens |

## 3. Methodology reference (not a paper — vendor documentation)

> Ansys Optics Application Gallery, *"Integrated microlens and grating coupler
> for photonic integrated circuits"* (Lumerical FDTD + Zemax OpticStudio).

This is the official Ansys workflow for exactly our problem, and it settles
several methodological questions the project brief leaves open. It confirms:

- The **Zemax/Lumerical boundary** the brief demands in §5: FDTD handles the
  grating; OpticStudio handles "macroscopic propagation and tolerancing" using
  **scalar diffraction theory** via POP.
- Coupling efficiency reported by POP is the **product of system efficiency and
  the overlap integral with the receiver mode** (worked example: 0.593864 ×
  0.66287 = 0.39365).
- Misalignment is introduced with **Coordinate Breaks**, swept with the
  **Universal Plot**.
- Its own worked geometry: 400 µm ROC microlens on 300 µm of silicon, ZBF plane
  rotated 5°, coupling into a 13 µm waist expanded-core fiber; a 2D sweep found
  peak efficiency near 500 µm ROC for a 300 µm lens-to-fiber distance.
- An explicit warning that the **"IN" direction (fiber → grating) cannot be
  accurately costed by POP alone**, because POP would need the grating's own mode
  as the receiver. This is why our model runs in the "OUT" direction.

Also used: Ansys KB *"Single-mode fiber coupling in OpticStudio"*, *"Exploring
Physical Optics Propagation (POP) in OpticStudio"*, *"ZBF Import/Export"*, and
*"Fiber-to-Chip Edge Coupler with a Microlens"*.

## 4. Selection

**Primary reference: P1 (Mangal et al., Opt. Express 29(5), 7601, 2021)**,
supported by P2 for numbers it abbreviates, P3/P4 for analytical ground truth,
P5 as the performance benchmark, and the Ansys application gallery as the
methodological template.

Selected because it is the only candidate where every number the Zemax model
needs is either printed in the paper or derivable from it, and because its own
design values came from OpticStudio — making Stage 4 a genuine reproduction
rather than a loose comparison.
